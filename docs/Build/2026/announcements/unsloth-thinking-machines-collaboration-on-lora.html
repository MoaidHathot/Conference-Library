About

Daniel Han explains LoRA as a fine-tuning approach where, instead of updating every weight, you add a small sliver of weights (an A matrix times a B matrix) added to the large model, updating only about 1% of weights for much less memory usage. He references a collaboration with Thinking Machines on a blog post ("LoRA Without Regret" [inferred]) showing that even a LoRA rank of 1 works well for specific use cases like math and coding. He cautions that hyperparameters must be chosen carefully, such as a learning rate 10 times larger than normal (2E-4) and an adjusted alpha/scaling.
