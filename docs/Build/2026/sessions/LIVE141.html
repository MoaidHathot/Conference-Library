Programming robots

Chip Huyen, John Maeda

Official description

AI is taking over the digital world, and it's exciting to see how AI can be used in the physical world. Once we have a robot, we need to figure out how to program it to do what we want. This demo shows how we can interface with multiple robots using the same API.

Summary

Overview

A fireside conversation in which Chip Huyen and John Maeda examine why programming robots in the physical world is far harder than automating digital tasks, even as large language models advance rapidly. The discussion frames robotic intelligence as a split between reasoning (now tractable with AI) and motion plus world-modeling (still unsolved), and offers guidance on where developers should focus.

Topics covered

  • The "ChatGPT moment" and the existential crisis it caused even for AI experts, attributed to humans mis-estimating how small accuracy gains translate into large jumps in usefulness [00:00:27m05sm10sp05sp10s]
  • Why the human body is harder to model than the human mind, including the lack of formal documentation, APIs, or predictable feedback loops in the physical world [00:02:58m05sm10sp05sp10s]
  • Robotic intelligence decomposed into reasoning/planning versus motion, per the cited Unitree CEO talk [00:05:26m05sm10sp05sp10s]
  • World modeling as a method to encode physical-environment information so AI can learn to operate within it, and the wave of well-funded startups pursuing it [00:06:33m05sm10sp05sp10s]
  • The debate between the vision-language-action (VLA) approach and the decoupled world-model approach, including the difficulty and cost of collecting action data tied to specific robot bodies [00:07:49m05sm10sp05sp10s]
  • On-demand motion generation versus pre-captured movements (e.g., robot Kung Fu demos) as a "Holy Grail" challenge [00:06:56m05sm10sp05sp10s]
  • Safety engineering: deliberately weak grip strength, distance-keeping rules, fall risk from heavy humanoids, and battery-death mid-motion leading to self-charging designs [00:11:04m05sm10sp05sp10s]
  • Career advice to filter the flood of news by focusing on a specific problem worth solving [00:09:39m05sm10sp05sp10s]
  • Two schools of robotics companies: general-purpose (data-heavy, capital-intensive) versus specialized single-task robots [00:13:26m05sm10sp05sp10s]
