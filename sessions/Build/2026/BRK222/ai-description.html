Introduction and Session Setup: The speaker, Lachlan Evenson, opens the presentation with high energy, addressing the audience at 00:00:01. He introduces himself as a long-time Kubernetes practitioner with over a decade of experience and sets the stage for a practical session on "Agentic AI on Kubernetes." Framing himself as an "honest practitioner," he explains that the session targets teams already using Kubernetes and exploring the best way to run AI workloads on it. The agenda, introduced around 00:01:19, covers what works today, common challenges, and the future direction of the ecosystem, promising demos, real-world use cases, and actionable takeaways. Lachlan emphasizes three focal points: understanding why Kubernetes is a strong substrate for AI, what operational stacks require, and showcasing how customers use these platforms in production.

Why Kubernetes for AI Workloads: Beginning at 00:03:35, Lachlan explores why Kubernetes is chosen for AI at scale. He compares managed AI platforms like Microsoft Foundry with self-managed solutions built on Kubernetes, explaining trade-offs between speed of deployment and fine-grained control. Using the iron triangle analogy around 00:06:00, he illustrates the balance between quality, speed, and cost for AI, showing how self-managed Kubernetes gives teams greater control over cost per token, data sovereignty, and open-source interoperability. He highlights that Kubernetes offers essential primitives — declarative resource management, heterogeneous scheduling, namespace isolation, and rich open-source tooling — making it ideal for composable AI environments despite added operational complexity. The section concludes by noting three distinct AI workload types — inference, training, and agentic systems — each requiring specialized infrastructure, introduced around 00:10:00.

Tooling and Demos: Inference, Training, and Deployment: Transitioning to practical guidance at 00:14:19, Lachlan presents a four-layer stack model: Kubernetes (substrate), inference and serving, training and fine-tuning, and agentic orchestration. Starting with inference, he introduces two open-source tools — AI Runway and Kaito — as Kubernetes-native solutions for deploying and managing AI inference workloads (00:17:00). He demonstrates with a recorded walkthrough by colleague Ralph, showing deployment of a large model using AI Runway’s GPU-fit planner, auto-scaling, and integrated storage caching. The demo, filmed around 00:20:00, showcases seamless setup, GPU scaling, and unified endpoints for inference using standard APIs. Moving upward, Lachlan introduces Ray and AnyScale on Azure for training and fine-tuning workloads. At 00:25:00 he demonstrates provisioning through the Azure Portal, launching Ray clusters, fine-tuning models with notebooks, monitoring vector performance, and deploying trained models in one interface — highlighting major productivity and observability improvements.

Agentic Systems and Orchestration: In the session’s later segment (00:35:05), Lachlan addresses the highest layer — agentic orchestration — which he notes is still rapidly evolving. He discusses the “skills” model for enabling agents to run discrete, permissioned tasks via the MCP protocol. Around 00:37:00, he announces the AKS MCP server enabling natural-language control of Kubernetes resources, such as provisioning clusters through voice or code interfaces. He introduces OpenClaw and the Agent Reference Stack for Kubernetes (AKARS), a secure sandbox for running AI agents with proper namespace, mesh, and identity isolation. A live demo around 00:39:00 shows deployment of OpenClaw, chatting with an AI agent using real-time data, and visualizing sandbox environments using Headlamp UI, demonstrating governance, observability, and secure experimentation.

Customer Success and New Platform Announcements: Moving toward conclusion, Lachlan highlights production use cases at 00:45:00. The Royal Bank of Canada uses Kaito with Azure Kubernetes Service (AKS) for self-service, compliant AI model deployment within secure perimeters. The self-driving firm Wave leverages Ray on AKS to train autonomous driving models at massive scale. He then unveils key Kubernetes platform announcements: AKS Automatic with managed system node pools, the new Azure Container Linux for secure, lightweight container hosts, AKS on Bare Metal for direct hardware access, and Fleet for cross-cluster orchestration and policy control, critical for scaling AI workloads across environments. These updates, detailed between 00:43:00–00:45:30, emphasize enhanced scalability, security, and efficiency.

Conclusions and Call to Action: Wrapping up around 00:49:00, Lachlan reiterates the key takeaways: begin with Kubernetes as the substrate, layer in MCP-based agent tooling, use open-source solutions like AI Runway, Kaito, and Ray, and adopt fleet management early as deployments grow. He encourages teams to explore the provided repos and build upon the shared demos and reference stacks. Closing enthusiastically, he thanks attendees, invites feedback and further discussion during the conference, and expresses excitement to see what practitioners will build next using agentic AI on Kubernetes. The session ends with applause and a call for innovation grounded in openness, composability, and practical application.
