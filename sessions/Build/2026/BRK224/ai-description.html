PepsiCo’s AI strategy starts with strong data foundations: The session stresses that AI projects fail without secure, governed, high-quality, and unified data. The company is moving away from data sprawl: PepsiCo identified challenges such as fragmented data, multiple copies, manual pipelines, poor interoperability, governance gaps, and vendor sprawl. The target is a unified enterprise data platform: PepsiCo’s vision is a centralized, lakehouse-style architecture where multiple teams can work from the same governed and integrated data foundation. Microsoft Fabric and Azure Databricks are central to the approach: These platforms help unify, govern, and prepare data for AI while improving security, scalability, and interoperability. AI-ready data must be curated and meaningful: Beyond unification, data must be clean, secure, contextual, and structured so agents can reason over it accurately. The business problem focuses on key account managers (KAMs): KAMs spend too much time gathering fragmented information from dashboards, reports, emails, and documents before customer meetings. PepsiCo built CAM 360 to simplify account preparation: The CAM 360 solution provides a single workflow where account plans, account health, insights, and preparation materials come together in one place. An Enterprise Data Foundation (EDF) makes the solution scalable: PepsiCo’s EDF includes governance, DevOps, standardized data models, reusable modules, and observability, enabling faster and more reliable AI development. The solution uses a multi-agent system: CAM 360 includes specialized agents such as an orchestrator, data analyst agent, persona and tactics agent, tracking agent, best practices agent, and debrief agent. The outcome is faster preparation and compounding intelligence: Instead of spending hours collecting information, users can ask one question and get insights in minutes, while the system continuously learns from meetings, debriefs, and account activity over time.
