Deploying AI models to production is only the start: Production deployment requires continuous monitoring, evaluation, and improvement to ensure models handle edge cases and perform reliably at scale. AI agents are highly token-intensive and expensive: Agents consume more tokens than standard chatbots because they perform iterative tool calls, reasoning, and policy validation, which increases operational cost. Post-training helps create faster, better, and cheaper agents: By learning from production traces and optimizing models, organizations can reduce cost while improving or maintaining quality over time. Fine-tuning teaches models task-specific and business-specific behavior: Fine-tuning helps models understand enterprise workflows, business rules, and tool usage, reducing errors and improving consistency in agent actions. Model selection should be guided by evaluation, not assumptions: Since many new models are constantly released, teams should use evaluations to identify which model best meets their quality and cost requirements. Custom evaluators are critical for defining success: Built-in benchmarks are helpful, but organizations must define their own success criteria and quality measures based on real business scenarios and data. Smaller open-source models can become strong cost-efficient alternatives: Although frontier models may perform better initially, smaller open-source models can be improved through supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement fine-tuning to reach high quality at much lower cost. Reinforcement fine-tuning improves decision paths, not just answers: Unlike supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement fine-tuning rewards the model for taking the right sequence of actions, making it especially useful for verifiable tasks like workflows, coding, and reasoning.
