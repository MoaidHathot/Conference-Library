Introduction and Overview: The presentation opens with Sarah Byrd, Microsoft’s Chief Product Officer for Responsible AI, introducing herself and colleague Sandeep at 00:00:08. They discuss the increasing need to monitor and control AI agents deployed at all stages, from early experimentation to production. Sarah highlights that agents are now being used widely but are also making costly mistakes (00:00:41). A global study found that 60% of agents access privileged data without proper authorization, causing sensitive information leaks (00:01:04). She frames the problem as one of trust and control—emphasizing the urgency of developing reliable monitoring mechanisms for responsible AI deployment.

Challenges in AI Agent Reliability: Sarah explains at 00:01:56 that agents can fail in four main ways: misunderstanding instructions due to confusion or malicious prompt injections, hallucinating incorrect information, leaking sensitive data, misusing tools, and exhibiting unexpected emergent behavior in multi-agent systems. She illustrates a “lethal trifecta” scenario (00:03:18) where an agent confuses internal and external contexts, exfiltrating sensitive data inadvertently. The solution, she argues, is implementing agent-specific safeguards that can detect and prevent undesired actions. At 00:04:43, Sarah introduces the four-step cycle Microsoft uses—identify risks, evaluate them, apply controls, and then continuously adjust based on real-world data—to build responsible AI systems.

Evaluation Tools and ASSERT Announcement: Moving into practical demonstrations, Sandeep introduces a banking manager agent built using the LangGraph framework at 00:06:04. Sarah and Sandeep use this agent to illustrate risk evaluation, highlighting issues such as prompt injection, hallucination, and unauthorized transactions (00:07:22). They launch Microsoft’s new open-source tool ASSERT at 00:10:18, designed to automate risk evaluation by converting broad responsible AI principles into concrete tests. ASSERT converts natural language policy definitions into evaluative taxonomies and test cases (00:13:02), streamlining a process that previously took weeks into minutes. A demo reveals how ASSERT flags policy violations in the banking agent and quantifies performance, reducing evaluation times drastically.

Agent Control Specification (ACS) and Guardrails: To address controlling agent actions, Sarah unveils the Agent Control Specification (ACS) framework at 00:25:00. While ASSERT identifies risks, ACS enforces guardrails across different system layers—inputs, tools, and outputs. It integrates with the existing Agent Governance Toolkit (AGT) (00:26:25), offering a standardized way to enforce behavior regardless of development framework. Through demos, Sandeep shows how declarative policies in ACS prevent actions like sharing Social Security numbers (00:28:20). Comparative metrics reveal dramatic improvements: error rates dropped from 28% baseline to 0% for simple prompts and to 10% for complex multi-turn interactions (00:30:28). Together, ASSERT and ACS form a feedback loop that detects, regulates, and monitors agent behavior within cloud and production environments.

Continuous Evaluations and Emerging Research: At 00:37:03, Sandeep presents the concept of continuous evaluations—an upgrade to ASSERT using reinforcement learning–based adaptive attackers that simulate ongoing threats. These self-improving evaluators not only test resilience but generate new adversarial cases to strengthen agents over time. Sarah adds that these evaluation results feed continuous learning systems to retrain or refine prompts, harnesses, and even model weights. They also highlight Microsoft’s work on information integrity, showing how imperceptible watermarks and C2PA metadata enable traceability of AI-generated content (00:41:11). Additional initiatives include secure data labeling in GitHub CLI and Fabric (00:42:29), and ongoing research on multi-agent safety, reflecting Microsoft’s proactive approach to the next wave of AI applications.

Conclusion and Call to Action: Wrapping up around 00:45:45, Sarah emphasizes open collaboration, community contribution, and transparency as the foundation of trusted AI. She invites developers to join the ASSERT and ACS open-source communities, urging everyone to contribute evaluations and controls. Her closing message reaffirms that building trustworthy AI depends on collective effort—developers must integrate responsible practices from design through deployment to ensure that AI systems are safe, accountable, and beneficial for society at large.
