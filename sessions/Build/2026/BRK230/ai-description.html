Introduction to Building Smart AI Systems: The session opens at 00:00:00 with Yina welcoming viewers to “Building Smart AI Systems in Foundry.” She introduces herself and Naomi, explaining their roles in Microsoft Foundry’s product and model operations. They frame the talk around the rapid evolution of AI, where new models and tools appear constantly, making continuous improvement essential. A year ago it was simple to deploy a chatbot, but systems now must be scalable, cost-effective, and adaptive to changing models and contexts. This evolving complexity sets the stage for demonstrating how Microsoft Foundry supports teams to build resilient, self-improving AI solutions at scale.

Understanding Microsoft Foundry’s Capabilities and Model Ecosystem: Around 00:05:00–00:10:00, the presenters dive into model selection and the Foundry platform. Developers are encouraged to treat model choice as a task-driven decision, not a one-size-fits-all choice. Foundry’s catalog, with more than 11,000 models, includes flagship collaborations with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Hugging Face, and new additions like Claude on Azure and Microsoft’s own multimodal models (image, code, audio). Purpose-built models are highlighted, such as Aurora 1.5 for geospatial applications and NVIDIA’s physical AI models. Foundry Labs from Microsoft Research offers cutting-edge experimentation while maintaining production-grade infrastructure. This segment emphasizes Foundry’s breadth — from frontier models to domain-specialized tools — unified under a governed, consistent API.

Evaluation and Optimization Framework with Practical Demo: From 00:13:00 onward, Yina and Naomi demonstrate scenario-based optimization using a “tree planning” or travel app example. Developers begin by defining success criteria such as correctness and policy compliance before prototyping. They run initial tests with a single large model (GPT-4.1) and measure results with built-in Foundry evaluators, discovering inefficiencies in cost and quality (“using a Ferrari for a grocery run”). Through model decomposition and routing, Foundry’s automatic or custom routers distribute tasks among small, efficient models, drastically improving performance. Visualizations show reduced latency and cost-per-task. This iterative “hill climbing” workflow demonstrates the importance of starting with evaluation-first thinking and progressively optimizing system architecture rather than simply picking cheaper models.

Advanced Evaluations, Rubrics, and Fine-Tuning: Midway through (00:25:00–00:37:00), Foundry’s evaluation catalog is presented, showing built-in and custom evaluators based on prompts, code, or rubrics. Rubric-based evaluation, newly announced, automatically infers performance dimensions from agent definitions and weights compliance or correctness accordingly. Developers can run custom evaluators to measure adherence to corporate policies or intent resolution. Code snippets illustrate how the system benchmarks each run with detailed tracing and clustering of agent behavior. They then introduce fine-tuning and distillation to embed domain-specific knowledge, such as travel policy rules, into smaller, cheaper models while maintaining quality. Foundry offers managed fine-tuning, serverless APIs for hyperparameter control, and full reinforcement learning options — all integrated into a streamlined, monitored deployment workflow.

Operational Excellence and Continuous Improvement: In the final section (00:40:00–00:45:00), the discussion shifts to optimization across cost, latency, and quality. Teams learn that architectural design — such as caching, batch inference, structured outputs, and token budgeting — drives larger savings than simply choosing cheaper models. Foundry introduces “explicit prompt caching” with Azure Context Cache for private, persistent performance improvements. Operational discipline follows: tracing, evaluating, and monitoring AI systems through Foundry’s portal and Azure Monitor. Observability ensures full end-to-end control of prompts, agents, and cost metrics. In closing, Yina reiterates the “hill climbing” philosophy — starting with clear success definitions, decomposing tasks into microservice-like components, and continuously improving system robustness. The presenters invite developers to build smarter, governed AI solutions with Microsoft Foundry via foundryai.azure.com, join the Discord community, and continue learning through related sessions on post-training and responsible AI.
